JPEG, PNG, WebP or AVIF: Which Wallpaper Format to Use

Last updated: 22 November 2026

Wallpaper files come in several formats, and the differences matter more than most people expect — particularly when a file that looks fine on your computer turns soft or refuses to set on your phone.

JPEG

The default for photographs and for almost every wallpaper you will download. Small files, universal support, and quality that is excellent at high settings. The weakness is that JPEG is lossy: every time you edit and re-save, quality drops a little more.

For a wallpaper you are not editing, JPEG at high quality is the right choice. If you are resizing and re-saving repeatedly, work from the original each time rather than saving over the same file.

PNG

Lossless, supports transparency, and produces much larger files. For wallpapers, PNG matters in one specific case: artwork with flat colours, hard edges and text, where JPEG's compression produces visible smudging around edges.

If you are setting a logo, a poster, or line art as your wallpaper, PNG will look cleaner. For a photograph, PNG gives you a much larger file for no visible benefit.

WebP

Google's format, now supported by every current Android version and by iOS since 14. It typically produces files 25 to 35 percent smaller than JPEG at equivalent quality, which is why most wallpaper sites have moved to it.

The practical issue: older Android versions and some gallery apps do not recognise it. If a wallpaper downloads but will not appear in your gallery, this is usually why. Convert to JPEG or update the gallery app.

AVIF and HEIC

Both are newer, both compress better than JPEG, and both are inconsistent in support. HEIC is what iPhones shoot by default. AVIF is increasingly used on websites.

If you download an AVIF wallpaper and your phone will not set it, that is a support gap, not a corrupt file. Convert it. Most online converters handle both formats.

GIF

Avoid it for wallpapers. GIF is limited to 256 colours, produces banding on gradients, and the files are large for what you get. An animated GIF wallpaper will look worse than the same animation as a video, and it will be bigger.

Video formats for live wallpapers

Live wallpapers are usually MP4 with H.264 encoding. This is the most compatible combination by a wide margin. HEVC (H.265) produces smaller files and is supported on most recent phones, but older devices may refuse it.

If a live wallpaper file plays on your computer but not your phone, check the codec before assuming the file is broken.

What format to use, in one line

Photographic wallpaper: JPEG or WebP. Artwork with flat colour or text: PNG. Live wallpaper: MP4 with H.264. Everything else: convert it.

A note on "HD" and "4K" labels

These labels on wallpaper sites are unreliable. A file can be labelled 4K and be a 1080-pixel image that has been upscaled. The only number that matters is the actual pixel dimensions, which you can check in any gallery app's file details. If the width is smaller than your screen's width, it will be soft regardless of what the filename says.

How to check what format a file actually is

Filenames lie. A file named wallpaper.jpg can be a WebP file that has been renamed, which is why it fails to open in an older gallery app. On Android, open the file's details from the gallery or a file manager and look at the MIME type rather than the extension. On a computer, most image viewers show the real format in their properties panel.

If the MIME type does not match the extension, renaming it back is usually enough to make it work.

Re-saving without losing quality

Every JPEG save loses a little quality. If you need to resize a wallpaper, resize the original once and keep that as your master copy. Repeatedly opening, resizing and saving the same file compounds the loss, and after several rounds the softness becomes visible on a phone screen.

For anything you plan to edit more than once, keep a PNG or a lossless copy as the master and export a JPEG only for the final version you actually set as a wallpaper.
