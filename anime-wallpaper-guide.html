The Anime Wallpaper Guide

Resolution, cropping, categories and how to pick something you will not get bored of in a week.

Home › Anime wallpaper guide

A phone wallpaper is the single image you look at more than any other. Getting it right is mostly about three things: the right resolution, the right composition for your screen, and picking something you will not get bored of in a week. This guide covers all three, plus how to find what you actually want in AniPix.

What makes a good phone wallpaper

Good wallpapers share a few practical qualities, and none of them are about the art being impressive.

  • It fits the screen. A wallpaper made for a wide monitor gets cropped badly on a tall phone. Portrait art, or art that survives a vertical crop, always looks better.
  • It leaves room for icons. Busy wallpapers with important detail in the top or bottom third fight with your app icons and clock. The best wallpapers have a quieter area where the interface sits.
  • It has a readable contrast level. If your icon labels are white and the wallpaper is bright, you will struggle to read them every day.
  • It is not too detailed at thumbnail size. A wallpaper that looks great full-screen but becomes visual noise behind icons is the most common mistake.

The eight categories, and what each is good for

Anime

Character art, key visuals and iconic scenes from popular shows. This is the largest category in AniPix, and it is where most people start. Character close-ups work well because the face gives the eye a natural focus point, leaving the rest of the screen calm.

Games

Key art, character wallpapers and screenshots from popular titles, cropped and colour-tuned for phone screens. Game key art tends to be compositionally strong already, which makes it forgiving.

Gods & mythology

Devotional and mythological artwork — deities, temples and traditional imagery in high detail. These are usually rich in colour and ornament, so they suit phones where you keep few icons on the home screen.

Nature

Mountains, forests, oceans and skies. If you want a break from characters, this is the calmest category, and the one that ages best — a good landscape does not stop being good.

Space

Galaxies, nebulae, planets and real astrophotography. Dark space wallpapers are especially practical: they are easy on the eyes at night and they make white icon labels very readable.

AI art

Original generated artwork — surreal landscapes, dragons and fantasy scenes you will not find elsewhere. Because AI art is not bound to an existing character design, it tends to have unusual compositions, which is either the appeal or the problem depending on what you want.

Resolution and cropping

Every wallpaper in AniPix is prepared at 1080×1920, which is a standard tall phone aspect ratio. That covers the vast majority of Android phones well. If your screen is taller (many modern phones are closer to 20:9), the app will crop slightly at the top and bottom when you set it, which is normal and rarely noticeable on a well-composed image.

If a specific wallpaper matters to you and the crop cuts something important, most phones let you pinch and adjust the framing when you set a wallpaper. Use that rather than accepting the default crop.

Dark wallpapers vs bright ones

There is a practical difference beyond taste. On OLED screens — which most mid-range and flagship phones now use — pure black pixels are switched off entirely rather than dimmed. A mostly black wallpaper therefore uses measurably less power than a bright one, and on a large OLED panel the difference is real over a full day.

Dark wallpapers also make white icon labels and widgets far easier to read, and they are gentler at night. Bright wallpapers, on the other hand, can look better in daylight and suit phones with a light theme. If your phone is set to a light theme with dark text, a bright wallpaper is often the better pairing.

Matching a wallpaper to your home screen

A useful rule: the busier your home screen, the simpler the wallpaper should be. If you keep twenty icons and three widgets on your first page, a detailed character wallpaper will disappear behind them and look like clutter. If you keep four icons and a clock, you can afford a busy wallpaper because there is nothing competing with it.

The same applies to the lock screen. The lock screen usually has a large clock and a couple of notifications, so wallpapers with a clear subject placed slightly off-centre tend to work best there.

How to find a specific character

AniPix's search is built around the way people actually type. It understands aliases and forgives typos, which matters more than it sounds:

  • Type jjk and it finds Jujutsu Kaisen.
  • Type itatchi — the common misspelling — and Itachi's wallpapers still appear.
  • Short forms, series names and character names all work as starting points.

If a search returns nothing, try the series name rather than the character, or a shorter fragment of the name. Searching for a full name with correct spelling is not required.

Static or live?

Static wallpapers are the safe default: no battery cost, no animation to distract you, and they look identical every time you unlock. Live wallpapers add movement and look genuinely good, but they use more power and are best suited to phones with larger batteries. Our live wallpaper guide covers how to set them and when to avoid them.

Keeping your library fresh

The fastest way to get bored of a wallpaper is to never change it. AniPix adds new wallpapers daily and puts a hand-picked pick on the Today tab each morning, which is a low-effort way to rotate what you are looking at. Favourites are worth using too — saving the five or six you actually like makes switching between them a two-tap job rather than a scroll through thousands.
