Wallpaper Size Calculator: What Resolution Your Phone Needs

Last updated: 22 November 2026

Phone screens are not all the same shape, and a wallpaper that looks sharp on one phone can look soft or badly cropped on another. This page explains the numbers and includes a calculator that tells you exactly what size to use for your device.

Why resolution is not the whole story

A wallpaper has to satisfy two separate requirements: it needs to be at least as many pixels as the screen, and it needs to match the screen's aspect ratio. Miss the first and it looks soft. Miss the second and it gets cropped or stretched.

Most phones now are close to a 9:20 ratio, which is much taller than the 9:16 that most wallpaper images are made in. That gap is why so many wallpapers get cut.

The calculator

Reading the result

"At least width by height" is the minimum. Below that, the phone scales the image up and you lose sharpness. Matching it exactly is fine for most people.

"Ideally double" matters on high-density screens. A 1080-wide image on a 1080-wide screen is one image pixel per physical pixel, which is sharp. But when you set it as a scrolling home screen wallpaper, the launcher needs extra width to scroll, so it stretches the image horizontally — and that is where softness appears.

The 16:9 note is the important one for most people. If you download a wallpaper made for a desktop monitor, it is 16:9. On a phone that is 9:20, you either crop the sides heavily or accept a squashed image. Neither is good.

How to find your phone's resolution

On Android: Settings → About phone → All specs → Resolution. Some phones show a different "HD+" or "FHD+" label instead of the raw number — those mean 720p and 1080p respectively.

On iPhone: the numbers are fixed per model. Search your model name plus "resolution" and you will get the exact figures, or use the calculator above with the values for your device.

The one setting that changes everything

If your launcher scrolls the wallpaper between pages, it needs roughly 1.5 to 2 times the screen width. A portrait image will be stretched or cropped to fill that. Turning wallpaper scrolling off is the single biggest improvement most people can make to how a wallpaper looks.

Why the same wallpaper looks different on two phones

Two phones can both be described as "1080p" and still show the same image differently, because 1080p only fixes the width. A 1080 by 2400 phone and a 1080 by 1920 phone have different heights, and the taller one crops more from the top and bottom of a 16:9 image.

This is why a wallpaper that looks perfect on an older phone can look zoomed in on a new one, even though both are the same width. The aspect ratio moved, not the resolution.

Screen density and why it matters

Density is the number of physical pixels per inch. Two phones with the same pixel resolution but different physical sizes have different densities, so an image that is one-pixel-per-pixel on a small screen is stretched across more physical area on a large one.

In practice: if you are choosing between two files of the same image, take the larger one. Android will downscale a big image cleanly. It cannot invent detail when upscaling a small one.
