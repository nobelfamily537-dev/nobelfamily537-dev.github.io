How to Set a Wallpaper on Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO (HyperOS)

Last updated: 22 November 2026

Xiaomi phones — Redmi, POCO and Mi models — run HyperOS now, which replaced MIUI. The wallpaper flow is similar but the menu names changed, and a few behaviours are specific to Xiaomi. Here is what actually happens on a current HyperOS build.

The basic flow

Open Gallery, open the image, tap More at the bottom, then Set as wallpaper. You will be asked whether you want it on the lock screen, the home screen, or both. Choosing both lets you adjust the crop separately for each, which most people skip and then regret.

The alternate route is Settings → Wallpaper → Gallery. This is the one to use if the Gallery option is missing or the crop behaves strangely.

The scrolling home screen problem

MIUI and HyperOS scroll the home screen wallpaper horizontally as you swipe between pages. When you set one image for both screens, the launcher crops it wider than the screen to give itself room to scroll.

Two consequences:

  • A portrait wallpaper gets zoomed in, cutting the top and bottom.
  • The lock screen shows only the centre of that wider crop, so it looks different from what you set.

The fix is either to set the two screens separately with different crops, or to turn off wallpaper scrolling. On HyperOS the scrolling toggle lives in the launcher settings rather than the wallpaper menu, which is why people cannot find it.

Carousel wallpaper — Xiaomi's best-kept feature

In Settings → Wallpaper → Carousel wallpaper, HyperOS can rotate through a set of images on the lock screen, changing each time you unlock. You choose the folder or album. This is genuinely useful and almost nobody uses it.

One caveat: carousel wallpapers work on the lock screen, not the home screen. If you want rotation on both, you need a live wallpaper app.

Super wallpapers

Xiaomi ships a set of animated 3D "super wallpapers" (the Earth and Mars ones are the well-known pair). They are beautiful and they are also among the heaviest wallpapers you can run — expect a measurable battery cost, especially on the always-on display.

If your phone runs warm or the battery drops faster after you set one, that is the cause. Switching to a static wallpaper is the fastest test.

Live wallpapers that will not animate

Third-party live wallpaper apps on HyperOS have one recurring failure: the animation plays in the app preview, then the home screen shows a still image. This is almost always the system picker applying the app's static preview instead of the live service.

Fix it from inside the app, not from Settings. Open the app, open the wallpaper, and press the app's own "Set wallpaper" button. Grant the "display over other apps" permission when asked — MIUI blocks live wallpapers without it, and the prompt is easy to dismiss by accident.

Battery saver kills live wallpapers

HyperOS has aggressive battery management. When battery saver turns on, live wallpapers are usually suspended and you get a frozen image. There is no setting that fully prevents this; the only reliable workaround is to exclude the wallpaper app from battery optimisation in Settings → Apps → Manage apps → [app] → Battery saver → No restrictions.

Wallpaper resolution on Xiaomi screens

Most current Redmi and POCO phones are 1080 by 2400 or 1220 by 2712. A 1080 by 1920 wallpaper will be scaled and cropped. It usually looks acceptable, but if you want it sharp, match or exceed the screen's native height.

To check your exact resolution: Settings → About phone → All specs → Resolution.

Two things that silently override your wallpaper

  • Themes. Applying a theme replaces the wallpaper with the theme's own asset. If your wallpaper keeps reverting, a theme is the reason.
  • Second space / dual apps. A separate space has its own wallpaper. Setting it in the main space does nothing there.

Both are quick to check and both waste a lot of people's time before they are found.
