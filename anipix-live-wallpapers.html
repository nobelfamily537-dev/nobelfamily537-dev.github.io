AniPix live wallpapers are animated anime wallpapers that keep playing behind your home screen instead of sitting still. They are the difference between a picture of a character and a character who actually moves. Every live wallpaper in the app is a real video wallpaper — a short clip that loops behind your home screen and app drawer, not a static image with a subtle effect layered on top.

This page covers the live wallpapers inside AniPix: what they are, how to set one, which phones support them, and what they actually cost you in battery. If you want a general, app-independent guide to how Android live wallpapers work, our blog has a separate full article on setting live wallpapers on Android.

What a live wallpaper actually is

Android has supported live wallpapers since 2010. Unlike a normal image, a live wallpaper is a small service that draws to the screen continuously — which is why it can animate, react, or loop a video. The system treats it differently from a static image, and that difference is the source of both its appeal and its battery cost.

In AniPix, live wallpapers are real video wallpapers: a short clip that loops behind your home screen and app drawer. They are not static images with a subtle effect added — they are the moving thing you see.

How to set a live wallpaper in AniPix

  1. Open AniPix and go to the Live category, or use search to find a specific one.
  2. Tap the live wallpaper you want to preview it. It will start playing.
  3. Tap Set wallpaper (or the apply icon).
  4. Choose Home screen, Lock screen, or Both. Live wallpapers generally work best on the home screen; on some phones the lock screen does not animate.
  5. Confirm. You will be returned to your home screen and the wallpaper will start playing.

If the app offers to open your phone's live wallpaper picker instead, that is normal — Android sometimes requires the system picker to confirm. Select AniPix from the list and tap Set wallpaper.

Setting one on different phone brands

Android's live wallpaper flow varies by manufacturer. These are the usual paths:

Samsung (One UI)

Long-press an empty area of the home screen → Wallpaper and style → Change wallpapers → choose Live wallpapers from the top tabs → pick AniPix. Samsung separates live wallpapers from static ones, so if you only see images, look for the live tab.

Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO (MIUI, HyperOS)

Long-press the home screen → Wallpaper → Live wallpapers → select AniPix. On some MIUI versions the live option is inside Themes rather than the wallpaper menu.

Google Pixel / stock Android

Long-press the home screen → Wallpaper & style → Change wallpaper → Live wallpapers → AniPix.

OnePlus / Oppo / Realme (ColorOS, OxygenOS)

Long-press the home screen → Wallpapers → Live wallpapers → AniPix. On ColorOS 12 and later the option is sometimes labelled Dynamic wallpaper.

Other launchers

If you use a third-party launcher, open its settings and look for Wallpaper → Live wallpaper. A small number of launchers — particularly very minimal ones — simply do not support live wallpapers at all, and no app can work around that.

Battery: what to actually expect

This is the part most guides exaggerate in both directions. The honest answer is that a live wallpaper does use more power than a static image, but the amount depends heavily on three things:

  • What is on screen. A live wallpaper only animates while it is visible. When you are inside an app, it is not being drawn.
  • How bright it is. A dark, slow-moving live wallpaper costs far less than a bright, fast one.
  • Your screen type. On OLED, dark content uses less power because black pixels switch off.

In practice, most people notice a small difference — not a dramatic one. If you are already ending the day at 10% battery, a bright live wallpaper will make that worse. If you end the day at 50%, you will probably not notice it at all.

If you want the effect without the cost, use a dark live wallpaper, or switch to a static wallpaper and keep the live one for weekends.

When a live wallpaper is the wrong choice

  • You need maximum battery life — for travel, long days out, or an older phone with a worn battery.
  • Your phone heats up easily — continuous drawing adds to that.
  • You use a minimal launcher that does not support them.
  • You are sensitive to motion — some people find a constantly moving background distracting rather than pleasant, and that is a perfectly good reason to skip them.

Troubleshooting

The wallpaper sets but does not move

Some launchers accept the wallpaper but display only its first frame. Try switching to your phone's stock launcher temporarily to confirm the wallpaper itself works; if it animates there, the issue is the launcher.

It stops after a while

Aggressive battery optimisation can pause background services, including live wallpapers. In Settings → Battery → Battery optimisation, find AniPix and set it to Unrestricted or Don't optimise.

The video stutters

Usually a slow connection on first load. Let it play once on Wi-Fi so it caches, then it should loop smoothly.

“Live wallpapers not supported”

Your launcher does not support them. Install or switch to a launcher that does — the stock launcher on almost every phone works.

Live or static — a simple rule

Use a live wallpaper if your phone has a decent battery and you like movement. Use a static one if you are often away from a charger, or if you simply want your home screen to stay out of the way. Both are good choices; the point is to make it deliberately rather than by accident.
